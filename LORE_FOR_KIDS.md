# The Story of the Quiet World

*(A bedtime version of the world's history. Read aloud if you can. The hard names are still here, but everything else is simple.)*

---

## A long, long time ago

A very long time ago — longer than anyone alive can remember, longer than the oldest book — there were people called **the Heleth**.

Nobody knows what the Heleth looked like. We do not have any pictures of them. We do not have any of their bones. Some old teachers think the Heleth were not really people at all — that they were more like a song that kept singing, even when no one was around to hear it.

What we *do* know is that the Heleth were very, very clever. They built things that we still cannot understand today. They built **machines that made light without fire**. They built **stones that could heal a cut**. They built **engines as big as cathedrals** that hummed a low, steady note all day and all night, and this hum kept the whole world warm and bright and working properly.

The Heleth called this energy **stillfire**, because it was a fire that did not move. It just *was*. Like a held note in a song.

And then, one day, the Heleth went away. Nobody knows where. They left all their machines behind, still humming, still working. They simply stopped being here.

The machines kept singing for thousands of years.

---

## The big, bright Empire

Eventually, new people — people like us — found the Heleth's machines.

A clever warrior-king named **Khelior** found one of the biggest engines buried under his palace. He did not understand how it worked, but he learned how to *use* it. Soon his children learned, and their children, and their children's children. They called themselves **Chord-Wardens** — people who could "read" the stillfire and turn it on and off and shape it into useful things.

This was the start of **the Empire of Vaelthar**. It was a wonderful, wonderful empire. It lasted eight hundred years.

In the Empire, knights rode dogs made of metal scales that glowed in the dark. Ships could *fly* — real ships, with sails, that went up into the clouds and crossed whole oceans before bedtime. The biggest cathedral in the capital city could send a beam of light up into space, where twelve other cities lived in the sky like a crown around the world.

It was the brightest time anyone had ever known.

---

## The morning everything went quiet

But one winter morning, three hundred years ago, something terrible happened.

In one single moment — between one breath and the next — **every single one of the Heleth's machines stopped humming**. The whole world. At the same time. Nobody knows why.

The flying ships fell out of the sky.
The twelve cities in space broke apart and rained down on the sea.
The lights everywhere went out.

People call that morning **the Long Quiet**, because what most people remember is not the noise of the cities falling. What they remember is the *silence afterward*. The held note that everyone had heard their whole life — and their parents had heard, and their grandparents had heard — was just… gone.

And out of the broken machines, **strange things started crawling**. Things that were a little bit alive and a little bit not. Things that used to be people, sometimes. Things that used to be a polishing-drone or a guard-dog or a kitchen-robot, sometimes. Now they were sad and angry and they did not know they had ever been anything else.

We call them **the Stilled**.

---

## The brave woman who started over

After the Long Quiet, the world was very dark and very scared. Most people just tried to find food and not get hurt.

But a young knight named **Mira Velour** — she was only twenty-three years old — stood up and said:

> *"We do not know what is going to happen. We do not know if the lights will ever come back. We do not know if anyone is listening to us at all. But somebody has to walk back into the dark places and try to fix them. So I will go. And anyone who wants to come with me, please come."*

A few people came with her. Then a few more. Then a lot.

She called them **the Verge** — because they live on the edge between the living world and the silent one, and they are the ones who walk back and forth.

The Verge is the family you belong to. That is who you play as.

---

## What we do

Three hundred years later, the Verge is still here. We are not very many — only about eight thousand brothers and sisters across the whole world — but we are still here, and we are still doing the work.

We **go into ruins** and find things the Empire left behind.
We **fight the Stilled** when they come out where they are not supposed to.
We **fix the broken engines** when we can.
We **carry the lost lights home**, one little spark at a time.

It is not a fast job. It is probably not going to be finished in your lifetime, or your grandchildren's lifetime. But it is **important** work, and somebody has to do it, and that somebody is us.

---

## What kind of hero are you?

Here is the best part. When you join the Verge, **nobody is going to tell you what kind of hero you have to be.**

Do you want to swing a big sword? You can. Pick one up.
Do you want to shoot a bow? You can. Pick one up.
Do you want to learn the old Heleth songs and make stillfire come out of your hands? You can do that too. Find a focus-stone. Practice.

You can be all three in the same day, if you want. You can change your mind tomorrow. The Verge has a saying:

> *"The gear you carry is the hero you are today. Tomorrow you may carry different gear and be a different hero. Both of them are you."*

Some heroes get really, really good at one thing. Some heroes get pretty good at many things. **Both kinds of hero are welcome.** The world needs both.

---

## The rules

There are only three rules you have to follow when you are part of the Verge. They are very serious. Bell-Warden Vossward — our leader right now — does not joke about them.

1. **If you find a broken Heleth engine, you must not leave it open.** Close the door behind you. Always.
2. **You must not sell the Empire's old things to the Silent Ord.** They are nice people, mostly, but they want to *break* the engines, and we do not.
3. **If you ever meet one of the Stilled, and it says a name from the Empire — like "I am Empress Theresia" — you have to tell us right away.** No matter where you are. No matter what time it is. Promise.

That is all.

---

## Now go

The world is dim, and it is a little scary, and there are old, old things in the ruins that have been waiting a long, long time.

But here is the thing about a dim world.

**Even a small light is very, very bright.**

You are a small light. So is the person standing next to you. So is the next one, and the next, and the next. And together — even if there are only a few of us, and we are tired, and we are scared, and we are not sure if anyone is going to remember our names — together we are bright enough.

That is the whole story.

Now go put on your boots. We have work to do.

> *"Walk gently. Speak kindly. Hit the Stilled hard, but only if they swing first. And when you find a small light, bring it home."*
> — Bell-Warden Mira Velour, on the day she founded the Verge
