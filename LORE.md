## The Heleth

Before recorded history there were **the Heleth** — a vanished civilization whose name in their own tongue meant *the ones who heard.* What survives of them is mostly mathematical: glyph-equations carved into mountain faces, geometries that hum when touched, indestructible plates etched with what may be sheet music or city-plans.

No skeletons of the Heleth have ever been found. No images. No graves. Some scholars argue they did not have bodies — that they were a sustained music, or many musics, written into the structure of stone and sky. The most common theory in **the Chordhouse** before its destruction was that the Heleth had **ascended into resonance**, becoming the harmonic patterns their own engines were tuned to.

What is certain:
- They mastered **stillfire** — the manipulation of a steady, structured energy that runs through the world like a held note.
- They left behind **chord-engines**: sealed devices the size of cathedrals, hand-sized **chord-cells**, and microscopic **chord-sand** used in alchemy.
- They vanished entirely between five and eight thousand years before the founding of the Empire of Vaelthar.

The Heleth left no warnings. No final inscriptions. No instructions. They simply stopped, and the geometry they had built kept singing without them for thousands of years.

## The Empire of Vaelthar

The Empire arose 1,100 years ago from the city-states of the **Astral Plains**, unified under the warlord-philosopher **Khelior the First**, who discovered an intact chord-engine beneath his ancestral palace. He did not understand it, but the first generation of **Chord-Wardens** — mathematicians who learned to "read" the stillfire patterns — slowly developed methods of drawing power from it.

For nearly eight centuries the Empire grew. Knights rode battle-hounds plated in stillfire-conductive scale. Skyships, called **upcraft**, crossed continents in a day. The **Cathedral of the Held Note** in **Vaelm-High**, the imperial capital, could project a beam of stillfire visible from beyond the atmosphere on clear nights. **The Chordhouse** trained four thousand scholars at any given time.

The Empire's heraldry: **a crown floating over a single black bell, mid-toll.** It signified mortal sovereignty over a power that rang, but did not rule, the world.

At its peak Vaelthar held:
- The three continents of **Vaelthar**, **Os-Karn**, and **Drumin'al**.
- The twelve **Sister-Spires** — orbital stations that ringed the world like a fixed crown of lights, named for the twelve apostles of Khelior's faith.
- Six undersea engine-cities in the **Held Sea**.
- An estimated 220 million subjects.

**Empress Theresia VII** ruled in the final years — said to be a brilliant mathematician, a poor diplomat, and a worse mother. Her court was the most ambitious in eight generations. Her Royal Chordhouse was performing experiments on the engines themselves: trying to understand not just *how* to use stillfire, but **what** it was, **where** it came from, and **whether the Heleth had been right to seal it.**

## The Long Quiet

The eleventh year of Empress Theresia VII. The winter solstice. **Year 0 of the Quiet Calendar.**

At dawn, every chord-engine on the planet — and the twelve in orbit — fell silent in the same instant. Not powered down. **Silent.** As if something had reached in and pinched the held note out of the air.

The cascade was immediate:
- **Upcraft fell.** Tens of thousands of skyships, across every continent. Most onto cities.
- **The Sister-Spires fell.** The twelve orbital stations, weighing millions of tons each, broke apart in the atmosphere and rained burning fragments onto the seas and lowlands.
- **Engine-cities collapsed.** Without stillfire to maintain their structural geometry, the undersea cities crumpled. An estimated four million dead in the first hour.
- **The Cathedral of the Held Note in Vaelm-High** — the imperial seat — was the first place from which **the Quiet-Born** began to emerge. The first of them climbed out of the cathedral's dead chord-engine before nightfall on Day 1.

By Day 7, no central government remained. By Day 30, no continent was unified. By the end of Year 1, an estimated 70% of the world's population was dead, and **the Stilled** had been seen on every continent.

The theological consequences were as severe as the material. Imperial doctrine had taught that stillfire was a divine gift and the Heleth had been the first servants of the **Held Note**. The Long Quiet implied one of two things: either the gods had withdrawn their favor, or — worse — **the gods had been the chord-engines all along, and now they were dead.**

The Silent Ord was founded six months later on the second interpretation.

## The Present Age

It is now **Quiet Year 312.**

The world is fragmented into **shard-kingdoms** — duchies, theocracies, mercantile city-states, and warlord territories — each clinging to whatever scraps of the old Empire they can defend. Power is rationed because stillfire is rationed; only a few chord-engines still function, mostly partial, and the knowledge to repair them is largely lost.

What remains worldwide:
- **Roughly 30 million** humans, down from 220.
- **Stannar**, the largest free-city (~400,000 people), built around the only fully functional Heleth forge.
- The **Silent Citadel** of the Silent Ord, holding the entire northern peninsula.
- **The Verge**, a coalition of recoverers and warrior-bellringers roughly 8,000 strong, headquartered at **the Tolling Keep** in central Vaelthar.
- Scattered free-towns, lord-holds, hermitages, raider-camps.
- The ruins. Each one full of things best left sealed. Each one too valuable to leave alone.

Heroes are not born; they are conscripts of necessity. The Verge does not recruit the strong; it recruits **the willing.**

## Factions

### The Verge *(player faction)*
**Heraldry:** A crown floating over a cracked black bell.
**Stated purpose:** Recover lost stillfire, push back the Stilled, one day relight Vaelm-High.
**Headquarters:** The Tolling Keep.
**Strength:** ~8,000 operators across all shard-kingdoms.

The Verge is not a kingdom and holds no sovereign territory. It operates as a chartered guild — accepting commissions from any shard-kingdom willing to pay, with a standing policy of always taking stillfire-recovery jobs even at a loss. This makes them the unofficial first responders for ruin-incursions and Stilled outbreaks.

The Verge is divided into three branches:
- **The Bell** — combat operators. Most members.
- **The Reliquary** — scholars and artificers who study what's recovered.
- **The Quieting** — specialists who contain or destroy corrupted chord-engines.

### The Brass-Houses of Stannar
**Heraldry:** Three iron rings interlinked.
**Stated purpose:** Profit, allegedly.
**Headquarters:** Stannar (the city itself is the seat).

Twelve merchant-noble houses run the largest surviving Heleth forge. Each House rotates the Lord-Forgemaster position annually. They sell to anyone, politicize nothing publicly, and are rumored to fund every side of every war.

It is widely whispered — though never confirmed — that the Brass-Houses keep a **living chord-engine** hidden beneath their central treasury. The Verge has investigated three times. None of the investigators returned.

### The Silent Ord
**Heraldry:** A black flame over white linen.
**Stated purpose:** Accept the Quiet. Reject stillfire. Atone.
**Headquarters:** The Silent Citadel.

A theocratic state founded six months after the Long Quiet. Doctrine: **the chord-engines were false gods, and humanity is being purged for worshipping them.** The only path to grace is to forsake stillfire entirely and accept the dark.

The Silent Ord burns oil lanterns instead of using cells. Its inquisitors hunt anyone who restores or uses stillfire technology. Its penitents undergo **the Iron Atonement** — sewn into iron plating, dosed on consecrated chord-sand, sent into the dark to die for the Ord's collective sin. (These are the **Silent-Brutes** the Verge encounters.)

The Ord is not stupid, and not without honor. Many of its priests are genuine ascetics. But its doctrine is incompatible with the Verge's mission, and conflict between the two is constant.

### The Stilled *(not a faction — a category)*
The corrupted. Anything — human, animal, Heleth construct, even building — that emerges from a dead chord-engine in a changed and hostile form. The Stilled are not one species. They are not one mind. They are a **category**, defined by what made them: the silence in the engines.

Some are barely-functioning automata. Some are men and women who survived the initial Long Quiet and were *changed* by extended exposure. Some are things that climbed out of the engines from somewhere else entirely — from wherever the Heleth had been trying to keep them.

The worst of them remember being people. The very worst remember being **Empresses.**

### Minor factions
- **The Held-Sea Folk** — descendants of the undersea engine-city survivors. Strange, beautiful, slightly alien.
- **The Free Companies** — mercenary bands; not the Verge but adjacent.
- **The Sundered Crowns** — former imperial nobility still claiming legitimate succession. Mostly pretending in old castles.
- **The Black Cartographers** — independent ruin-mappers. They sell maps to anyone. Including monsters.

## Geography (global)

> *Anything labeled "Heleth site" is provisional — they shift, in ways no one understands.*

- **The Shardlands** (central Vaelthar) — broken heart of the old Empire. Most ruins, most Verge activity. Temperate; brutal winters.
- **Stannar** (eastern coast of Vaelthar) — largest surviving city. Walled, lantern-lit, prosperous in a tense way.
- **The Silent Citadel** (northern peninsula) — theocratic stronghold of the Silent Ord. Cold. Hostile to outsiders. Beautiful in a terrible way.
- **The Held Sea** (southern ocean) — the seas where the Sister-Spires fell. Twelve crash-sites, each a mile-wide crater on the sea floor. The Held-Sea Folk live among them. *Things* live deeper.
- **Os-Karn** (southern continent) — largely depopulated. Warlord territories. The Verge has minimal presence. Said to hold the highest concentration of intact Heleth sites.
- **Drumin'al** (western continent) — lost. Communication broke during the Long Quiet and never resumed. Three Verge expeditions sent. None returned. **Off-limits** to recovery missions pending intelligence.
- **The Astral Plains** (origin of the Empire) — now a depopulated grassland. Khelior's palace site buried under three centuries of dust. Pilgrims still visit. Bandits prey on them.
- **Vaelm-High** (imperial capital, ruin) — **closed.** The Long Quiet's epicenter. Nobody who has entered has come back unchanged.

## The Verge (player faction)

### Founding
Founded in **Quiet Year 4** by **Bell-Warden Mira Velour**, a surviving knight of the Cathedral of the Held Note. She was twenty-three when she watched Vaelm-High fall. By twenty-five she had drafted the Verge's founding charter:

> *"We do not serve the Empire, for the Empire is dead. We do not serve the gods, for we do not know if they ever lived. We serve the next ruin, the next door, the next note returned to the silence. If we are wrong, may we at least be wrong while moving forward."*

Velour died in Quiet Year 47 at the Battle of Eight Bridges. She is buried at the Tolling Keep. Her sword — **The Last Tolling**, a one-handed blade with a stillfire edge that hums when blood is near — remains in service. Whoever currently holds the rank of Bell-Warden bears it.

### Structure
- **Bell-Warden** — the Verge's leader. Currently **Aldric Vossward** (Quiet Year 312).
- **Verge-Captains** — regional commanders.
- **Bell-Knights** — branch officers (one per branch).
- **Operators** — the rank-and-file. The player begins here.

### Operator rights and duties
A field operator may:
- Refuse any single contract.
- Keep 60% of recovered relics by appraised value; the Verge takes 40%.
- Quit the Verge with thirty days' notice.

A field operator must:
- Never knowingly leave a corrupted chord-engine unsealed.
- Never sell stillfire technology to the Silent Ord.
- **Report any of the Stilled that speaks an imperial name,** immediately, by any means.

The third clause is non-negotiable. Bell-Warden Vossward has executed three operators in his term for violating it.

### Character philosophy — *the Verge has no classes*

The Verge does not divide its operators by class. There are no "warriors," "mages," "rogues," or "priests" as separate career tracks. Every operator is taught **the same fundamentals** at the Tolling Keep — sword, bow, basic stillfire resonance, field medicine, ruin-mapping — and is then **free to specialize through the gear they carry and the stats they raise.**

**Anyone can wield anything.**
- A heavy operator can pick up a bow and shoot it. Less effectively than someone who raised DEX, but the bow will fire.
- A scholarly operator can swing a two-handed axe. Less effectively than someone who raised STR, but the axe will swing.
- A focus-staff can be used as a club by anyone, and a steel longsword can serve as a stillfire conduit if a chord-cell is socketed into its hilt.

This is doctrine, not happenstance. Bell-Warden Mira Velour wrote it explicitly into the founding charter:

> *"The Empire fell because it asked each of its servants to be one thing only. The Mage could not fight. The Knight could not read. The Astromancer could not lift a sword. When the engines fell silent, no one could do anything but their assigned task — and their assigned task no longer existed. The Verge will not repeat this mistake. Every operator is a complete operator. The gear they hold is the role they play, today. Tomorrow they may hold different gear and play a different role. This is how we survive."*
> — *Founding Charter, Article IV*

In practice this means:
- **Stats matter more than class.** Six core attributes (STR, AGI, VIT, INT, DEX, LUK) shape what an operator is *good* at — but never restrict what they're *allowed* to do.
- **Gear is the role.** Equipping a bow makes you a ranged shooter for this fight. Switching to a focus-staff makes you a caster for the next. The Verge encourages every operator to carry two loadouts.
- **No skill trees lock you in.** Talents and abilities are tied to gear and stats, not to a chosen path at character creation. Raise INT and equip a staff later in life and you will cast as well as anyone who started there.
- **Builds emerge from play, not selection.** Specialization is the result of long practice with a single archetype, not a label you picked on Day 1.

The Silent Ord considers this doctrine heretical. *("If everyone is everything, no one is anything.")* The Verge considers the alternative a luxury the world can no longer afford.

## Stillfire — magic and technology

There is no "magic" in the modern sense — only **stillfire resonance**, the manipulation of Heleth power patterns through trained mind, consecrated tool, and proper resonance geometry.

### Chord-engines
The original Heleth power sources. They come in sizes from **great-chord engines** (the size of buildings, capable of powering cities) down to **chord-cells** (palm-sized, single-spell capacity) and **chord-sand** (microscopic, consumable, used in alchemy).

Great-chord engines are nearly all dead since the Long Quiet. A handful — Stannar's, the Tolling Keep's chapel, the suspected one beneath Vaelm-High — still function. Chord-cells are more common but draining; once depleted they cannot be recharged with current technology.

### Foci
Trained users cannot channel stillfire with empty hands. They need a **focus** — a prepared object (staff, prayer-stone, sigil-tattoo, weapon-mount) that shapes the resonance pattern. The focus is consumed slowly with use; even the best ones break after a few hundred castings.

### The cost
Every stillfire effect costs something:
- **Minor effects** (a flame, a small healing, a flash of light): cell charge or alchemical reagent.
- **Major effects** (combat spells, full healing, scrying): blood, fatigue, sometimes hours of life.
- **Great effects** (resurrection, sealing a corrupted engine, mass healing): **memory.** The caster forgets days, weeks, or whole years.

This is why the most powerful Chord-Wardens tend to be old and erratic. They cannot remember why they know what they know.

### Forbidden practices
The Verge forbids three things, on pain of expulsion:
1. **Naming the Stilled** — using stillfire to summon, control, or speak with anything that emerged from the engines.
2. **Note-binding** — what the Hush-Magisters did to survive the Long Quiet.
3. **Engine-walking** — entering an active chord-engine in the flesh. Three Verge operators have attempted this. None returned.

## Bestiary

### Husk-Crab *(template 0)*
**Level 1 · Friendly · Husk class · Servitor**
Beetle-sized servitor drones, originally used to polish cathedral floors and clean engine housings. After the Long Quiet, their directives decayed into simple territorial patrols. They scuttle in irregular pack formations under ruined plazas. **They do not attack unless attacked first.** Most operators kill them anyway — they sell well to artificers (the chitin contains trace chord-residue).

### Engine-Slime *(template 1)*
**Level 5 · Aggressive · Stilled class · Anomaly**
A semi-aware mass of Heleth coolant gel that began thinking during the Long Quiet. Whether it possesses true sentience or only the appearance of it is debated. What isn't debated: it dissolves leather, eats through armor at the seams, and is afraid of dry stone (it cannot move easily across non-porous surfaces).

### Iron-Hound *(template 2)*
**Level 10 · Aggressive · Stilled class · Construct**
The Empire's elite **Khelior-pattern battle-hound** — a Heleth-derived war construct with adaptive armor plating, scent-lock targeting, and a partial-AI command system. Used by imperial cavalry before the Long Quiet. Their handlers are three centuries dead. Their command systems decayed. Their directives now default to "kill anything that moves." They still wear the Empress's livery and salute imperial colors before attacking.

### Wayward Knight *(template 3)*
**Level 15 · Aggressive · Stilled class · Revenant**
A man-at-arms who died during the initial Long Quiet and got back up. Armor pristine. Sword still sharp. The body still remembers training drills — formal guards, the salute to a superior officer. The mind, what remains of it, may not know it died. Some have been observed standing at attention waiting for orders that will never come, indefinitely, for years.

The Verge treats Wayward Knights with reluctance. They were soldiers of the Empire. **Killing one is an act of mercy and should be performed with respect.**

### Silent-Brute *(template 4)*
**Level 20 · Aggressive · Living · Penitent**
A volunteer of the Silent Ord who underwent **the Iron Atonement**: sewn into iron plating, dosed on consecrated chord-sand, sent into the dark to die for the Ord's sins. Most are insane within a year. Most die within two. Some — a frightening minority — survive much longer and become living siege weapons, leading raid-parties of fanatic acolytes.

The Verge does not consider Silent-Brutes part of the Stilled — they are still alive, still nominally human, still capable in principle of being reasoned with. (Conditions which almost never obtain in combat.)

### Hush-Magister *(template 9, boss)*
**Level 50 · Aggressive · Stilled class · Note-Bound**
**One of the original Royal Chord-Wardens of Empress Theresia VII's court.** Survived the Long Quiet by binding their soul to a fragment of a dying chord-engine — in violation of all imperial law and all subsequent Verge doctrine. The result is something not quite alive, not quite dead, not quite mortal — a thinking, scheming entity that wields broken fragments of Heleth resonance, remembers its own name, and remembers **your line**, in some cases, by lineage.

The Hush-Magister will speak. It will negotiate. It will make offers. **It is lying.** Every successful kill of a Hush-Magister has been performed by an operator who refused to converse.

Four Hush-Magisters are believed extant, scattered across the continents. Killing one is a tier-1 Verge objective and earns a Bell-Knighthood.

## Named Figures

### Bell-Warden Aldric Vossward
Current leader of the Verge. Age 51. Former Bell-Knight of the Bell branch. Took command after the death of Bell-Warden Lyena Mors at the Siege of the Sevenfold Wood (Quiet Year 308). Pragmatic, cold, respected. Personally bears **The Last Tolling.**

### Bell-Knight Tenh Halloway
Head of the Reliquary branch. Eighty years old. Lost both legs to a Hush-Magister in his thirties; walks on Heleth-prosthetic stilts. The Verge's foremost living expert on great-chord engine function. Difficult, brilliant, and unkind to fools.

### Lord-Forgemaster Voren Aimes
Current rotating head of the Brass-Houses (Quiet Year 312). Diplomat. Charming. Owns a great deal of art. Suspected of negotiating in secret with the Silent Ord. The Verge has not been able to prove anything.

### High Cantor Vesthia
Spiritual leader of the Silent Ord. Old, frail, possibly senile. Probably the only person alive whose word can call off a Silent-Brute mid-attack. Has refused all communication with the Verge since Quiet Year 305.

### Hush-Magister Aurelyne
One of the four known surviving Hush-Magisters. Formerly Royal Chord-Warden Aurelyne Hass, court mathematician under Empress Theresia VII. Was twenty-eight at the time of the Long Quiet. Currently believed to operate from a sealed sub-vault beneath Vaelm-High's outer cathedral. Has been observed by surviving scouts. Has spoken to two of them. Both are dead now, by their own hand.

### The Long Mother
A figure reported by survivors of the Silent Citadel — a woman in white linen, very tall, who walks the inner gardens at night and speaks to the oil-lanterns. The Ord denies she exists. The Verge suspects she is either an unrecognized member of the Stilled or — worse — **the closest thing the Ord's doctrine has to a living goddess.**

## Calendar & Eras

- **Pre-Imperial Era** — undated. Heleth civilization and the long centuries of its aftermath.
- **Imperial Era** — Year 1 (founding by Khelior the First) to Year 803.
- **Quiet Year 0** — the year of the Long Quiet. Year 803 of the Imperial calendar.
- **Quiet Era** — present. **Quiet Year 312** is the current setting.

The Silent Ord keeps the **Penitent Calendar**, counted from a different solstice. Some scholars track both. Stannar uses the Quiet calendar but offsets fiscally by six months.

## Tone & Design Pillars

**Old.** Everything is built on top of something older. Every village has a Heleth stone in its well. Every sword has a name and a history of murder. Nothing is new.

**Hopeful, but barely.** The world is dimming, the silence is winning the long game, but there are still people walking back into ruins because someone has to. The Verge is a small bell, and small bells are still bells.

**Dangerous without being grimdark.** Bad things happen. Heroes die. Innocents die. The world does not apologize. But characters can still be kind to each other, still laugh, still find each other in the dark. The grimness is the backdrop, not the point.

**Strange.** The Heleth artifacts are alien. The Stilled are uncanny. Stillfire is a half-understood science. Things should never feel *normal* — even normal life should have an edge of "we don't really know how any of this works."

**Personal.** Players are not chosen. Not prophesied. Not destined. They are *volunteers*. The world owes them nothing. They walk into the dark because someone has to, and they decided to be the someone.

### Recurring themes
- **Knowledge as recovery, not invention.** Every spell, every tool, every weapon is rediscovered. Nothing is being made for the first time.
- **Faith without certainty.** The gods may be dead. The saints definitely are. What remains is the work.
- **Old voices in dead places.** The Empress remembers you. The cathedral remembers the song. The sword remembers its bearer. The dead are not silent; they are *very specifically and very disturbingly articulate.*
- **The cost of light.** Every stillfire effect costs something. Every relic recovered cost lives. Every shard of progress is paid for.
